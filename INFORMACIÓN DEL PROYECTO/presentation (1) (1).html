03Problem
⭐

Business Problem

Predict daily sales per product and branch to support centralized production planning.

📈

ML Problem

Supervised learning leading to regression / time-series forecasting.

⚠️

Challenge — Demand Is:

  • Highly variable day-to-day
  • Zero-inflated (many days with no sales per product)
  • Influenced by external factors (weather, holidays, quincena)

Context

We are trying to predict how many units of each product will be sold per day. The challenge is that the data is very noisy and has many zero values, which makes modeling more complex.

Why this matters: Over-production leads to waste; under-production leads to lost sales and empty shelves. A 7-day demand forecast allows production managers to place accurate orders weekly.

05Data Preparation

The dataset was prepared to ensure reliability for modeling. Missing values were handled using zero imputation, where missing records were interpreted as days with no sales rather than unknown values.

This approach was chosen because it preserves the natural structure of daily sales data and avoids introducing artificial values that could bias the model.

Feature engineering was applied by creating rolling sales variables (7, 30, 90 days), allowing the model to capture demand patterns and trends.

La Carreta — special case: This branch does not open on Sundays. Sunday rows are removed at load time before any analysis. Closure-aware data preparation ensures no "fake zeros" pollute training.
Tier 1 fix applied across all branches: Rolling features audited for target leakage. qty_roll_7/30 reconstructed as shift(1).rolling(N).mean() to guarantee day-t is excluded from its own rolling mean.

Preparation Pipeline

  1. Remove is_modifier = True rows — add-ons inflate counts
  2. Filter to top 10 products per branch by total volume
  3. Reindex to continuous daily calendar; fill missing days with quantity = 0
  4. Forward-fill exogenous features: tavg, cold_or_warm
  5. Detect branch closure days (no product sold on that date)
  6. Audit and reconstruct rolling features — no target leakage
  7. Label-encode categoricals for LightGBM: day_enc, item_enc, holtype_enc

Why lag_7 Is the Dominant Predictor

Bakery demand repeats weekly — conchas sell ~3× more on weekends than weekdays, every week. lag_7 captures exactly this: "what did we sell this same weekday last week?" Rolling averages blur the weekday signal by averaging across different days. SHAP analysis across all branches confirms lag_7 as the single strongest predictor in every LightGBM model, followed by item_enc (product identity) and day_enc (weekday).

lag_7 → #1 predictor item_enc → #2 day_enc → #3 tavg → weather signal is_quincena → pay cycles lag_365 → annual cycle
07Sales Behavior
  • Daily sales are highly skewed — many zero and low values, a few large spikes driven by weekdays, paydays, and events.
  • lag_7 (same weekday last week) is the single strongest demand signal — SHAP confirms it as the #1 feature across every branch and product.
  • CONCHA VAINILLA is the top-selling SKU at all 7 branches, making it the most critical product to forecast accurately.
  • Quincena effect: demand spikes noticeably around the 15th and 30th of each month — Mexican paydays drive discretionary bakery spending.
Why this matters for model selection: Standard regression assumes normally distributed errors. Zero-inflated, right-skewed count data requires specialized approaches. LightGBM uses Tweedie loss (variance_power=1.5) — positioned between Poisson (1) and Gamma (2) — which naturally handles this distribution.

09Models

ARIMA

AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average

Models a series as a function of its past values and past forecast errors. One model per product. auto_arima selects best (p,d,q) via AIC. Handles trend via differencing.

Exog: is_quincena, is_holiday, tavg, cold_or_warm_num

SARIMA

Seasonal ARIMA — m = 7 (weekly cycle)

Extends ARIMA with seasonal terms (P,D,Q)₇. Explicitly learns that Monday's demand relates to last Monday's. Most important pattern in bakery data.

Orders selected via auto_arima(seasonal=True, m=7)

Prophet

Meta additive decomposition — y(t) = g(t) + s(t) + h(t) + ε

Decomposes demand into trend + seasonality + holiday effects. Multiplicative seasonality. Built-in Mexican holiday handler with ±1 day window.

Regressors: tavg, cold_or_warm_num, is_quincena, lag_365

LightGBM

Gradient boosted trees — one model, all 10 products

Uses ALL features simultaneously. Tweedie objective (power=1.5) for count data. Leaf-wise growth, GOSS sampling. item_enc allows single model to serve 10 products.

800 trees · lr=0.03 · 63 leaves · Tweedie loss

XGBoost 35 models

One model per (branch, product) pair = 35 models total (7 branches × 5 products). Last 90 days held out for validation. Conservative params to reduce overfit on small per-product datasets.

objective: reg:squarederror
n_estimators: 1000 # early stopping
max_depth: 5 # moderate complexity
learning_rate: 0.05
subsample: 0.8 # row sampling
colsample_bytree: 0.8
early_stopping_rounds: 50
eval_metric: rmse

Ensemble (Prophet + LightGBM)

Simple average of Prophet and LightGBM forecasts. Two diverse model families: Prophet (additive decomposition + seasonality priors) + LightGBM (tree-based, non-linear interactions). Averaging reduces variance when models make different kinds of errors.

Backtesting setup — ARIMA · SARIMA · Prophet · LightGBM · Ensemble

12Conclusion

What Was Built

  • 6 model families evaluated across 7 branches
  • 35 XGBoost models (branch × product pairs)
  • Evidence notebooks with top 10 products each
  • 7-day and 30-day backtests + walk-forward CV
  • Scenario planning (quincena, holidays, weather)
  • Production-ready 7-day demand forecast pipeline

Key Findings

  • lag_7 is the single strongest predictor in every model
  • LightGBM and XGBoost are competitive with classical time-series on this data
  • Ensemble (Prophet + LightGBM) often outperforms both individually
  • Credi Club is the hardest branch to model (sparse, volatile)
  • Plaza Nativa achieves the best WAPE — most predictable demand
  • Weekly aggregation significantly improves apparent accuracy

Limitations

  • New products: no lag features available — models unreliable
  • Operational closures filled with 0 can bias time-series models
  • WAPE > 40% for all 35 XGBoost models — bakery demand is inherently volatile
  • LightGBM residuals likely have autocorrelation (tree models ignore temporal ordering)
  • Sudden menu changes, promotions, or external events not captured

Recommended Next Steps

  • Deploy LightGBM or Ensemble as production model
  • Automate weekly retraining on new POS data
  • Add promotion flag as an exogenous feature
  • Monitor walk-forward CV accuracy week-over-week
  • Expand to remaining products beyond top 10
The system provides Panem with a data-driven foundation for production planning —
replacing intuition with forecasts validated across 4 years of history.
