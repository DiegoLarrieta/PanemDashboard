03Project Context

Panem operates in a high-uncertainty fresh-food environment

Operating reality

  • Multiple branches with fresh bakery and food products.
  • Production planning happens before demand is fully visible.
  • Each branch has different demand behavior and product mix.
  • Fresh products have limited shelf life.

Why this matters

Forecast error has a direct operational cost. Too much production increases waste; too little production creates stockouts and lost sales.

The project adds a data baseline to planning that currently depends on experience and recent sales memory.

13Model Interpretation

The models are useful for planning, not for automatic exact production

Model target

  • Units sold by product, branch, and date.
  • Chronological validation to avoid future leakage.
  • Baseline: Naive lag-7, meaning the same weekday from the previous week.
  • Metrics: MAE, RMSE, MAPE, WAPE, and accuracy within ±20%.

Managerial interpretation

MAPE helped compare forecast error in business terms, but daily bakery demand remains volatile. Predictions should be reviewed with safety buffers before final production.

18Conclusions

Panem can move from intuition-based planning to data-supported production decisions

Conclusions

  • Demand is branch-specific, product-specific, and highly seasonal.
  • Forecasts are valuable, but daily bakery demand still requires human review.
  • The dashboard bridges analytics and daily operations.

Recommendations

  • Use the forecast as a baseline, not as an automatic final quantity.
  • Apply safety buffers for high-stockout-risk products.
  • Collect actuals, waste, and overrides to improve the system over time.
